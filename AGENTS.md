## Falling-sand game at `/pour`

The owner's latest gameplay feedback supersedes the original ridge prototype. Read `docs/stone-sand-plan.md`, `docs/status.md`, and `docs/architecture.md` before substantive work. The intended experience is quiet, slow sand accumulating on a stone triangle. There is **one brush action**, available to multiple fingers: brush through that sand, dislodge it, and shape the deposits that later sand runs over. Do not reintroduce Draw/Rub modes or invisible player-drawn barriers.

Sand adheres weakly to rough stone and more strongly to other attached sand. Brushing leaves a visible sticky trace: repeated passes stack its strength, and it fades with a six-second half-life. Traces grip across a stroke while allowing downhill sliding along it; drawing uphill must not reverse gravity. Nearly horizontal marks have reduced effective grip so a broad finger cannot make an invisible dam. Mark depth, adhesion and guidance read the same decaying field. The inlet must drain gently, with weaker attachment and temporary upper bonds. Some sand stays on the stone; missed sand remains physically present in piles at the bottom. Relaxed Free Pour is the default. Challenge is optional and must be versioned when its physics, supply, targets, or scoring conditions change. Historical measurements and superseded plans are provenance, not current gates.

Dust is released fully above the board from an invisible opening that slowly sways horizontally. Do not draw a nozzle, source line or marker on the triangle. The opening uses a smooth 60-second cycle and simulation time, starting on the first pour; it keeps moving while emission pauses and freezes while hidden or finished. A fresh simulation restores its phase; Free Pour then runs its short preparation described below. Offscreen grains still count toward supply, retained mass and the cap.

The aperture spans 34 logical units with 120 lanes. Emission varies around a nominal 120 grains/s: seeded smooth noise gives slow swells and smaller ripples, with occasional short sputter clusters and actual gaps. Keep grain velocity gentle (16-unit/s cap); varying the flow changes arrival density. `source-flow.js` samples seed and source time without consuming particle randomness or mutating state. Pauses/caps never store a backlog. Setting `sourceFlowVariation: 0` gives a steady control fixture. The 4,800-grain challenge has no fixed pour duration and uses `stone-offering-v8` for the revised brush conditions.

The current gameplay pass is documented in `docs/gameplay-review.md`. A 14-unit finger displaces sand into banks and carries some along the swipe (`brushCarry: 0.6`, brush speed 9). Physical samples are spaced by travelled distance across events, independently per pointer. End/cancel flushes its residual path; ending one finger must preserve the others. Holding still cannot repeatedly pump sand. Flushes can clear Finish eligibility, and finished games cannot be edited by a late lift. Keep all real grains and dependent bond releases.

Free Pour uses a fresh seeded shower and stone weathering for each run. It simulates nine seconds of the actual offscreen source before entry, so there is material to touch immediately. It keeps every resulting grain/count and the real clock/phase; never fabricate a bed on the face or rewind particle time. After preparation only discovery is cleared, with stationary grain reveal caches invalidated. Challenge remains empty at preparation with fixed seed 48 and all 4,800 grains. `?debug&seed=48` repeats a free run for checks; normal free resets vary. Tuning for entry and discovery completion is in `src/config/free-pour.js`.

Free Pour has a quiet discovery readout. At 82% opacity-weighted stone coverage it reads “Stone found” and Reset becomes “New stone.” Play continues with no modal, timer, streak, auto-reset or loss of the creation. Coverage excludes off-stone texels and never decays. Brushing uncovers more than passive rain (radius 10, pass gain 0.8; grain rate 0.016/contact-second). This goal is a design hypothesis for human review, not proof of enjoyment or retention.

The entire stone starts invisible, including its rim. Fingers and sand progressively reveal only the areas they touch. Keep discovery in its own persistent logical field, separate from the decaying grease and physical adhesion. Repeated contact deepens visibility; uncovered areas remain visible for the current run. Reset/mode changes hide the stone again, while Begin, resize, hidden recovery and grease decay preserve discovery. The artwork, rim and grease share the reveal mask; do not leak an untouched silhouette, shadow or outline. Discovery must not consume physics randomness or change motion/accounting.

Fine dust is revision 0.11.0: radius 0.13, exactly one fifth of 0.65, and nominal flow 120/s, exactly five times 24/s. The retained cap is 30,000; Challenge scales supply/targets to 4,800 and 1,800 / 3,000, preserving its approximate pour duration and ratio. See `docs/fine-dust.md`. Air uses a separate seeded spatial field and size-dependent drag, follows source time, is sheltered over the stone and tapers near the floor. It affects loose awake grains only. Preserve seed independence, gentle speed bounds and sleeping/bound material.

Use occupied-bin clearing and separate moving/fixed collision grids. Invalidate the fixed partition on anchor/release, sleeping-ground wake/sleep transitions, capacity changes and retained-array compaction. Sleeping face grains must still query supports for adhesion. Compare cached grids with forced rebuilds in tests. Rendering rasterizes every actual grain into a transparent subpixel disk layer; never enlarge fine grains to a one-pixel minimum, hide particles, aggregate their physics or discard piles for speed. Per-cell grease decay is cached for one simulation timestamp and invalidated by painting.

The triangle is worn old stone, the grains are pale dust, and brushed traces read as dark greasy finger marks. Layering makes the marks darker; fading returns them toward the stone's color. Keep the physical grains legible over both stone and grease. The palette is defined by CSS custom properties in `src/styles.css`; `src/rendering/palette.js` reads the canvas colors when rebuilding cached artwork. Avoid green/sage coatings, luminous painted rails, invented lore glyphs and extra interface clutter. See `docs/visual-style.md` for references and current screenshots. Styling alone does not change the challenge identifier.

Use the owner's `src/assets/fonts/FringeV2.otf` for all game text, including Canvas bowl counts and notices. Buttons should feel like sand-blasted mechanical cassette-futurism hardware: recessed housings, worn bronze key faces, physical bevels, amber selection indicators and a visible depressed state. Keep semantic buttons, clear keyboard focus and at least 44 CSS pixels of touch height. No animated grit or extra labels/glyphs.

Small wakes on recently brushed real grains and brief bowl surface/rim responses should make actual movement and arrivals legible. Reduced motion omits those decorations. Material audio starts muted, creates its nodes only on Sound opt-in, and responds to actual brush contact/collection. Hide/mute silences and stops active voices; never replay past contacts on a new scene. Avoid fake reward particles, glowing rails, extra currencies or pressure mechanics.

## Code and simulation

- Use Vite, vanilla JavaScript ES modules, semantic HTML, CSS, Canvas 2D, and optional Web Audio. No framework or full physics engine.
- Keep simulation, seeded randomness, accounting, challenge/scoring, and sharing independent of the DOM. Rendering reads state; pointer input produces brush strokes.
- Use the fixed 160 × 200 logical field, fixed 60 Hz steps, three collision substeps and two contact passes per substep, stable spatial-hash ordering, and seeded randomness. Screen size/DPR must never change physics.
- Keep all tuning defaults in `src/config/`, with logical units. Keep motion substantially slower than the original 78-unit/s prototype. The owner explicitly requests five times the recent emission: 120 fine grains/s nominal, without faster motion.
- The physical supports are sand grains. Represent adhesion explicitly and release dependent attachments when a supporting bond is brushed away. A stroke moves/releases existing material; it never creates rail geometry or deletes grains.
- Keep missed grains in the simulation and render their resting piles. Count bowl collection once. Preserve `emitted = left + right + all retained grains`; retained grains include those above the board, on the stone and on the ground. `spill` counts retained grains marked as missed, so `emitted = left + right + spill + faceCount` also holds.
- A grain may sleep when resting. Brush edits, weathered bonds and moving contacts must wake affected material. Removing adhesion must not leave frozen unsupported descendants or sleeping loose grains. Never discard grains for performance.
- Prefer plain named functions/data over generic abstractions. Document nonobvious numerical behavior near the solver. Keep status, commands, tuning reports and constraints current.

## Interaction and feel

Use Pointer Events for touch, pen, and mouse. Capture and track every active pointer independently, including each previous stroke point. Releasing, cancelling or losing capture for one finger must not interrupt the others or connect their paths. Interpolate fast strokes and deposit trace strength by distance rather than event frequency. Brush the entire visible triangle, including its tip and bottom rim. A contact centred just outside an edge can still overlap it. Share the triangle geometry between rendering and interaction, clipping effects to the stone; bowls and the surrounding space remain outside. Show each contact footprint, keep controls at least 44 CSS pixels tall, and apply `touch-action: none` only to the canvas.

Preserve all grains, bonds, piles, counts, supply and traces through resize/rotation; suspend stepping while hidden and reset the frame clock on return. Stopping the pour only stops new emission; traces still decay. Trace time follows simulation time and freezes when hidden or finished. Reset/mode changes clear all traces and active contacts. No countdown, rushing cues, or catch-up bursts. Sound starts muted. Reduced motion removes decorative trails while retaining necessary sand movement and functional sticky traces.
